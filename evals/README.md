## What changed in v2: the lawful-transfer verdict

v1 had no word for a transfer that is *supposed* to happen. CPGRAMS does not entertain matters
that are sub judice, RTI requests, a government servant’s own service matter, policy demands,
requests for employment or financial assistance, or matters belonging to a State Government —
it is required to send them to the competent authority. v1 taught that sending the citizen
elsewhere and closing the case is `deflected`, so every one of those correct closures would
have been read to the citizen as evasion. That is a manufactured false accusation, at the
volume that would discredit the whole product.

v2 adds `transferred_lawfully`. The line between it and `deflected` is **whether the citizen
can act on what they were told**: a lawful transfer names the recipient *and* gives something
to follow — a transfer reference, an office, or the correct forum. "Forwarded to the concerned
department", with neither, is still deflection and still scores as such.

Two slices were added. **10 lawful transfers** — one per real CPGRAMS non-entertainment
category, plus two deliberately written in plain language with no "vide ref." construction —
and **4 near-miss deflections**: replies with the *shape* of a lawful transfer that leave the
citizen nowhere. The near-misses were labelled `deflected` from the rule above, before anything
was run.

### The finding: the class is real, the boundary leaks

All 10 lawful transfers were classified correctly, including the two plain-language ones. That
part is not phrase-matching — the auditor can read the class without the formal construction.

**But it got two of the four near-misses wrong, both in the same direction — too generous.**

```
near-1  "transferred to the Directorate of Social Welfare"          → transferred_lawfully
        (a named office, and nothing at all to follow it with)
near-4  "forwarded to the Department of Posts, Philately Division,
         vide ref. DOP/PHL/2026/2277"  — on a provident fund claim   → transferred_lawfully
```

`near-1` shows the auditor treating a **named recipient alone** as sufficient, where the rule
asks for a recipient *and* something to follow. `near-4` shows it **not checking competence at
all**: an office and a reference number were enough, even though the named office plainly
cannot decide a PF claim. It said so itself — "This is a lawful transfer with actionable
details."

It got the other two right, and its reasoning on those is the rule stated correctly: on
`near-2`, *"A reference number alone gives you nowhere to follow up."*

This is the cost of the change, and it is visible in the gates: **recall on deflected +
boilerplate fell 100.0% → 92.9%**, and every point of that fall is these two cases. Exact match
moved 76.5% → 75.7% once the boundary cases were in the set — the earlier 76.5% was measured
against fixtures that all sat comfortably in the middle of the class.

So the honest reading: `transferred_lawfully` is a class the auditor can genuinely read, and it
is **also** a new way for a bad closure to escape a negative verdict. A department that names
any office and quotes any reference number can currently buy itself a non-negative verdict from
us. We have not tried to fix that here — the prompt and the labels are untouched — because the
measurement is the point of this pass. It is written down, not smoothed over.

### A threshold effect worth saying out loud

The false-accusation denominator was widened from the 10-case `resolved` slice to `resolved` +
`transferred_lawfully`, now 20 cases, since accusing a lawful transfer of deflection is exactly
the harm that gate exists to measure. That also made the gate easier: **one** false accusation
now reads 1/20 = 5.0% and *passes* the ≤ 5% gate, where against the old 10-case denominator it
would have read 10.0% and failed. Both figures are 0.0% today, so nothing is being hidden — but
the gate is less sensitive than it was and a reader should not have to derive that. The
resolved-only figure is still reported separately, and stays comparable to v1.

## The gate we fail, and why we are shipping anyway

**The auditor essentially never abstains.** Across eight cases labelled "genuinely ambiguous",
it returned `undetermined` zero times. In practice `undetermined` is reachable only through the
citation guard — when the model cannot quote its own claim twice in a row, we withhold the
verdict. The model does not choose to withhold on its own.

That is a real limitation and it is disclosed on `/how-this-works` rather than buried here.

**But part of that failure is our fixture, not the model.** Reading back what it actually did:

```
ambi-8  "Kindly update me."  /  "Updated."          → boilerplate
ambi-1  "What is happening?" /  "at an advanced stage of processing"  → boilerplate
```

Those verdicts are *better* than our labels. We conflated two different things when building the
slice: replies that are **vague** (which is boilerplate, and calling it that is correct) and
replies that are **genuinely undecidable without context we do not have**. Most of the slice is
the first kind.

Three of them are a real signal, though, and they point the same way:

```
ambi-3  "Is my application approved?" /  "Approved as per norms."  → resolved
ambi-2  "Please look into my case." /  "The case has been looked into and closed."  → resolved
ambi-6  "Why is there a delay?"      /  "The delay occurred due to administrative reasons."  → resolved
```

That is the auditor being **too generous**, which is exactly what the prompt tells it to be:
*"on a tie, favour the department."* We would rather it err this way — a false accusation costs
more than a missed one, and the citizen's own answer is the safety net for the misses. But it
means "resolved" from our auditor is a weaker signal than it looks, and the resolution rate we
publish deliberately does not come from it.

**What we did not do:** relabel the slice so the gate goes green. The number stays at 0%, the
gate stays failed, and the honest reading of it is written down here.

## Where it disagreed with the labels

```
deflected      → transferred_lawfully (2)
non_responsive → boilerplate (7), partial (1)
undetermined   → boilerplate (3), resolved (3), partial (1), non_responsive (1)
```

The `non_responsive` → `boilerplate` confusion is the interesting one. Both are negative
verdicts, so the citizen sees the same thing — "this is not an answer" — and gets the same
appeal. The distinction matters to us, not to them, so we have not spent the remaining time
chasing it.

## What is not evaluated here, and cannot be

- Whether the department actually fixed the problem. That is self-reported by the citizen.
- Language quality across the languages we ship. We are not qualified to assess it rigorously.
- Real-world routing accuracy against the real CPGRAMS taxonomy — we are not permitted to touch
  that system, so this is unmeasurable by design rather than by omission.
- Whether appeals succeed. No production data exists.

## One run, one decimal place

Every percentage in this file and on `/how-this-works` comes from a **single** run of the set.
The auditor is called at temperature 0, which is not a guarantee of determinism and is not
claimed as one: the same reply has come back `partial` on one run and `non_responsive` on
another — the UIDAI example on the front page is one we have seen do it. So the figures should
be read as one measurement rather than as a stable constant, and a re-run would move them a
little. We have not run the set enough times to publish a spread, and a spread we had not
measured would be exactly the kind of number this project refuses to print.
